## 1. Business model

You build fast, custom-coded websites for owner-operated Dallas businesses that get customers from Google and phone calls, then keep earning from monthly care plans. The money is in the recurring care, not the one-time build.

### Ideal client profile

- Owner-operated, 1–15 employees, in business 2+ years (they have revenue and reviews but a weak or missing site)
- Gets jobs from Google search, Google Maps and phone calls, so a better site turns into booked work they can see
- Currently has no site, a Facebook-only presence, or an old Wix/GoDaddy site that is slow on phones
- The owner makes the decision alone (no committee, no marketing department)
- Inside a 30-minute drive so you can meet in person

### Top 5 Dallas niches to target first

| Rank | Niche | Why it fits | What their site needs most |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Home service trades: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage door, handyman | One job is worth $300–$5,000, so a $999 site pays for itself fast. Dallas heat keeps HVAC busy year-round | Click-to-call, service-area pages, quote form, reviews |
| 2 | Outdoor and cleaning crews: lawn care, landscaping, pressure washing, house cleaning, pool service | Many are owner-run crews living on Facebook and word of mouth; recurring customers | Quote form, before/after gallery, service area map |
| 3 | Barbers, salons, lash, brow and nail studios | Strong Instagram but weak sites; booking is the job to be done | Online booking embed, price list, gallery, Maps |
| 4 | Auto detailing, tint and independent repair shops | Customers search "near me" on phones; high-ticket packages | Package pricing, booking or quote form, reviews |
| 5 | Fitness: personal trainers, boxing, martial arts, yoga and Pilates studios | Need a trial-class or consult signup; owners already pay for marketing | Class schedule or booking, trial signup, coach bios |

**Default:** pick niches 1 and 3 for your first 30 days. Trades pay the most; studios decide fastest. Skip restaurants (thin margins, they live on Instagram and delivery apps) and doctors, dentists and lawyers (compliance and agency competition) until you have a portfolio.

**Optional edge:** Dallas County is roughly 40% Hispanic. If you can write and sell in Spanish, offer bilingual English/Spanish sites. Few freelancers do it well, and it is a real reason to pick you.

### Value proposition

> A fast, professional website that gets you more calls from Google, built in about 2 weeks by a local developer you can text, for less than an agency, and you own it.

Three proof points to repeat in every pitch:

1. **Faster sites.** Hand-coded sites load in about 1 second on a phone and score 90+ on Google PageSpeed. Show the score live next to their current site.
2. **Built for local search.** Google Business Profile setup, local business schema, service-area pages and review links are part of the build, not an upsell.
3. **A real person nearby.** You meet in person, answer texts the same day, and handle updates so the owner never logs into anything.

### Positioning

|  | Dallas agency | Wix / Squarespace DIY | You |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cost | $5,000–$15,000+ | $0 | From $499 |
| Monthly cost | $150–$500+ retainers | $17–$50 plan + the owner's time | $49, edits at $75/hr |
| Who builds it | Account manager, then a junior or offshore team | The owner, nights and weekends | You, directly |
| Speed on phones | Varies; often heavy WordPress | Often slow, generic templates | 90+ PageSpeed target |
| Turnaround | 6–12 weeks | Whenever the owner finds time | 2–3 weeks |
| Ownership | Often locked to their hosting | Locked to the platform | Client owns domain and accounts; code transferable |

**What to say against Wix:** "Wix is great if you have the time. My clients would rather spend that time on jobs. I build it, I keep it updated, and it's faster than a template."

**What to say against agencies:** "You get the same local SEO setup an agency does, from the person actually building it, for about a third of the price."

**What not to say:** don't lead with "I use AI." Clients buy outcomes (calls, bookings, a site they're proud of). Claude Code is how you deliver fast and cheap; it is not the pitch. If asked, be honest: "I use modern AI tools to code faster, and I review and test every line."

### What counts as a revision round

## 3. Monthly care plan

One care plan at $49 a month for hosting and upkeep. It includes no edit time: every edit is billed at $75/hr (below). Care is optional; any site you host must be on the plan, and a client who skips it gets a full handoff instead (section 4).

The care plan includes:

- Hosting on your Vercel account, SSL and uptime monitoring
- Form and booking checks, tested monthly
- Dependency and security updates, backups in GitHub
- Replies within 1 business day

Content edits (text, photos, hours, prices), monthly reports, Google Business Profile posts, new content and a yearly refresh aren't included; they're work beyond the plan (below).

**Default:** offer the care plan with every build. It's billed from launch day and renews monthly, with 30 days' notice to cancel, so recurring revenue starts the day the site goes live.

**Rules that protect your time:**

- Every edit, and any other work beyond the plan, is billed at $75/hr in 30-minute blocks, quoted before you start.
- Bill care plans on the same day each month by auto-charge (card or ACH) so you never chase payments.
- If a care-plan payment fails and isn't fixed within 15 days, pause edits and care work until it's paid. Never take the site offline for non-payment. If it's still unpaid 60 days after its due date, you may end the care plan by written notice and hand off the site (repo transfer, $150 handoff fee); the unpaid fees stay owed.
- Raise prices for new clients first; give existing clients 60 days' notice of any increase.

## 4. Client workflow: first contact to launch

Every project runs the same 9 stages, and no build work starts until the contract is signed and the deposit has cleared.

1. **First contact** (day 0): reply within 4 hours, book a 20-minute discovery call.
2. **Discovery call** (day 1–3): qualify, then send a proposal within 24 hours.
3. **Proposal + contract + deposit** (day 3–7): e-signed contract, deposit (50%).
4. **Intake form + kickoff** (day 7–10): client fills the intake form; you set up the repo and accounts.
5. **Content collection** (by a set date): turnaround clock starts only when content is complete.
6. **Build** (Launch 10, Growth 15 business days).
7. **Review rounds**: preview link, consolidated feedback, revisions.
8. **Final payment + launch**: final payment due before the domain goes live.
9. **Handoff + care plan**: walkthrough, logins doc, review request, 30-day check-in.

### Discovery call questions (20 minutes)

Business and goals:

1. Walk me through what you do and who your best customer is.
2. How do most new customers find you today? (Google, referrals, Facebook, Yelp, Nextdoor)
3. If the new site works, what happens? More calls, more bookings, higher-priced jobs, fewer "what do you charge?" calls?
4. Roughly what is one new customer worth to you? (This is how you justify the price.)
5. What areas or neighborhoods do you serve?

Current setup:

6. Do you have a website now? Who built it and who has the logins?
7. Do you own your domain? Which company is it registered with?
8. Do you have a Google Business Profile? How many reviews?
9. What do you use for booking, payments or quotes?

Fit and logistics:

10. Show me 2–3 websites you like, from any industry. What do you like about them?
11. Do you have a logo, photos of your work, and text about your services?
12. When do you want this live, and is anything driving that date?
13. Who besides you has a say in the decision?
14. Have you set a budget? (If they hesitate: "My packages start at $499. Most businesses like yours fit Growth, which starts at $999. While I build my portfolio, Launch starts at $499 for a 5-page site.")

**Red flags (walk away or price higher):** wants e-commerce with 100+ products, "just copy this competitor's site," asks for unlimited revisions, can't name who decides, pushes back on any deposit.

### Payment structure

**Default:** Launch and Growth are 50% deposit to book, 50% before launch.

- The deposit holds your calendar slot. It is fully refundable until the kickoff call and non-refundable after it. (Attorney to confirm this wording before first use.)
- Final payment is due before you point the domain at the new site. The site stays on a preview link until paid.
- Care plan auto-billing starts on launch day.

### Content collection

The biggest delay in this business is the client not sending content. Protect the schedule:

- Set a content due date in the contract, 7 days after kickoff.
- Send a shared folder (Google Drive) with subfolders: `logo`, `photos`, `reviews`, `text`.
- Send reminders on day 3 and day 6. On day 8, offer to write the copy for the next tier up or move them to the back of your queue.
- If content is more than 30 days late, the contract lets you invoice the final payment and launch with what you have.

### Build phase

- Day 1: send a 1-page "site plan" (page list, menu, main call to action per page). Get a thumbs-up before designing.
- Middle: share one in-progress preview link so there are no surprises.
- Keep a single feedback channel: email, or comments on the preview. Not text messages scattered across days.

### Review rounds

- Send the Vercel preview link with a short Loom video (2–3 minutes) walking through the site.
- Ask for **one consolidated list** of changes within 5 business days.
- Turn revisions around in 2–3 business days.
- After the final included round, changes move to the care plan or the hourly rate.

### Launch checklist

- [ ] Final payment received
- [ ] Pre-launch QA checklist passed (section 5)
- [ ] Client wrote "approved for launch" by email
- [ ] Domain added to the Vercel project; DNS records updated at the client's registrar
- [ ] HTTPS working on both `www` and the bare domain, one redirects to the other
- [ ] Old site's important URLs redirected (301) to the new pages
- [ ] Forms send a test submission to the client's real inbox
- [ ] Booking embed books and cancels a test appointment
- [ ] Google Search Console verified, sitemap submitted
- [ ] Google Business Profile website link updated to the new site
- [ ] Analytics shows your own test visit
- [ ] Launch announced to the client with the live link

### Handoff

- 30-minute walkthrough call (recorded): how to request edits, what the report shows, how to share the site.
- Send a "Your website" document: live URL, where the domain is registered, who to contact, what the care plan covers, renewal date.
- Give them a printed or digital QR code that links to their Google review page.
- Day 7 after launch: ask for the testimonial and Google review.
- Day 30: send the first results email (visits, calls, form submissions) and ask for one referral.
- If they decline a care plan: transfer the GitHub repo to their account and walk them through moving hosting. Charge a one-time $150 handoff fee. Keep hosting the site for 30 days after any care plan ends, then remove it from your Vercel account.

## 5. Technical build process

Build every client site from one starter template where all business facts live in a single content file, so Claude Code edits data and reuses components instead of writing each site from scratch. Target: a Growth site in 12–18 hours of your time.

### Stack defaults

| Need | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framework | Astro with Tailwind CSS | Static HTML output (fast, cheap to host), Markdown for blog posts, components reused across clients |
| Code hosting | GitHub, one organization for the business | Clean separation from personal repos; easy transfer to a client |
| Site hosting | Vercel Pro ($20/mo) | The free Hobby plan is for non-commercial use only, so client sites belong on Pro. One care plan covers it. Every branch gets a preview link for reviews |
| Forms | Web3Forms or Formspree free tier, with a honeypot field | No server code to maintain; upgrade only when a client gets heavy volume |
| Booking | Embed the client's existing tool (Square, Vagaro, Booksy, Calendly, Cal.com) | Never build booking yourself |
| Analytics | Vercel Web Analytics + Google Search Console | Privacy-friendly, no cookie banner needed for basic use |
| Reviews | Hand-copied real reviews in the content file + link to the Google review page | Third-party review widgets are slow and cost monthly |
| Images | WebP via Astro's image component, max 200 KB each | Keeps phone PageSpeed above 90 |

### Using Claude Code efficiently

1. **Every repo has a `CLAUDE.md`.** It holds the client's name, niche, tone, brand colors, pages, the "never do" list (no invented reviews, no medical or legal claims, no stock photos of fake staff) and the commands to run. Claude reads it every session, so you never re-explain.
2. **Start from data, not prompts.** Paste the intake form answers into `src/content/site.ts` first. Then ask Claude to build pages from that file. Facts stay correct and consistent.
3. **Use plan mode for anything bigger than one component.** Ask for a plan, correct it, then let it build. Cheaper than undoing a wrong build.
4. **Save repeat jobs as slash commands** in the template's `.claude/commands/`:
   - `/new-site`: fill `site.ts` from `docs/intake.md`, generate pages, set metadata and schema
   - `/add-page <name>`: create a page from the section templates and add it to nav, footer and sitemap
   - `/qa`: run the build, Lighthouse and link check, then report against the QA checklist below
   - `/handoff`: write the client's "Your website" doc from `site.ts`
5. **One task per session, one commit per task.** Short, focused sessions use fewer tokens and are easy to roll back.
6. **Have Claude check its own work visually.** Ask it to screenshot pages at 390px and 1440px with Playwright and fix what looks off.
7. **Improve the template, not just the site.** When you build a component worth reusing (e.g. a before/after slider), copy it back into the template repo the same week.
8. **You review every diff.** You are the one accountable to the client. Read what changed before you push.
9. **Claude builds, Codex fixes.** Claude hands security fixes, failing builds, dependency alerts and QA defects to Codex through the Codex plugin, and keeps building. Codex's rules are in the repo's `AGENTS.md`. Before you show a client, run `/codex:review` on the change, or `/codex:adversarial-review` when it touches forms or customer data.

**Default plan:** Claude Pro ($20/mo) to start. Move to Max (about $100/mo) once you regularly hit usage limits mid-build, usually around client 4 or 5.

### Design toolkit and build recipe

Adapted from the design guide "Cooked or Cracked: 5 free repos that make Claude design" (Henry Chua, tested 27 Sep 2026; a copy is in Google Drive, `extra/`). Its prices and its hosting (Cloudflare Pages) don't apply here: prices are sections 2 and 3, and hosting is Vercel Pro.

**The tools (all free).** Start a new Claude Code session after installing one, so Claude loads it. Where a tool's rules disagree with the client's brand or the template, the brand and the template win.

| Tool | What it does | When to use it | Install |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Taste (`design-taste-frontend`) | Design rules that stop the "AI look"; deals a researched art direction; finds openly licensed photos of the client's world, with credits | Every build | `npx skills add https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill --skill design-taste-frontend -a claude-code -y` (reviewed 2026-10-21: MIT licence; its scripts only search open image archives) |
| Playwright CLI | Opens the site in a real browser, clicks everything and takes screenshots | Every build, before anyone sees it | `npm install -g @playwright/cli` (installed) |
| Impeccable | Lists design problems on a finished page; `critique`, `audit`, `polish` | Every build, after version 1 and before launch | Claude Code plugin (installed) |
| Awesome DESIGN.md | Style files copied from big brands' sites | Only for a client with no look of their own; keep their colors | One folder from `github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md` |
| img2threejs | Rebuilds a photographed object as a 3D model | Only for a product customers would spin round (a sneaker, a bottle); never food | `github.com/img2threejs/img2threejs` (Python 3.10+) |

**The build, step by step** (inside the section 4 workflow, starting from the template):

1. Fill `site.ts` from the intake. Ask Claude to build version 1 from the template, "using the taste skill for every design decision", with colors taken from the client's logo.
2. Photos, in this order: the client's own; then openly licensed photos of their street, city or trade, with credits (Taste's archive search); AI-made images only for general scenes (a street, a texture), with the owner's written OK, and never as photos of their products, food or work (decisions log, 2026-10-21).
3. One signature scroll moment per site, tied to what the business does. Not a fade-up on every section.
4. Playwright: click every link and button, screenshot every section at 390px and 1440px and as an iPhone, and fix what's broken.
5. `/impeccable audit`. Check each finding before fixing it (many are false alarms), then run `/impeccable polish`.
6. The pre-launch QA checklist below, then show the owner.

**A scroll-played clip** (the guide's signature trick, and Trinity's own How it works section): render a short clip with the HyperFrames kit or from the client's own footage, encode it with a keyframe every 2 frames so seeking stays smooth, and let the page set the video's time from the scroll. The working component is `site/src/components/site/BuildClip.astro`; copy it into the template the first time a client site uses one.

### Pre-launch QA checklist

Run `/qa` first, then check by hand. Paste the results into `docs/qa-report.md`.

**Mobile**

- [ ] Checked at 360, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440px wide, no sideways scrolling
- [ ] Tested on a real iPhone (Safari) and a real Android phone (Chrome)
- [ ] Phone number is a `tel:` link; tap targets at least 44×44px
- [ ] Main call-to-action visible without scrolling on a phone

**Speed**

- [ ] Lighthouse mobile Performance 90+ (run 3 times, use the median)
- [ ] Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 s on mobile
- [ ] Every image WebP/AVIF, sized for its slot, under 200 KB; below-the-fold images lazy-loaded
- [ ] No unused third-party scripts

**SEO**

- [ ] Unique title (under 60 characters) and meta description (under 155) on every page
- [ ] Exactly one H1 per page, includes the service and city where natural
- [ ] LocalBusiness schema passes Google's Rich Results Test; name, address, phone match the Google Business Profile exactly
- [ ] `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` live; canonical URLs set; Open Graph image set
- [ ] Old URLs redirected; no broken links (link checker clean)

**Accessibility**

- [ ] Lighthouse Accessibility 95+
- [ ] Text contrast at least 4.5:1; every image has meaningful alt text (or empty alt if decorative)
- [ ] Whole site usable with keyboard only, visible focus outline
- [ ] Form fields have labels; errors are announced in text, not just color

**Forms and integrations**

- [ ] Contact and quote forms deliver to the client's inbox (check spam folder too)
- [ ] Spam protection on (honeypot) and a thank-you message or page shows
- [ ] Booking embed loads on mobile and a test booking works
- [ ] Map shows the right location; click-to-call and directions links work

**Content and legal**

- [ ] No placeholder text; spelling checked; hours, prices and phone confirmed with the client
- [ ] Every review is real and attributed as the client provided it
- [ ] Privacy policy page live (the site collects form data); footer shows the current year
- [ ] 404 page and favicon in place

## 6. Business setup checklist

Form a Texas LLC before you sign your first contract, open a separate bank account, and use one tool for invoices and recurring care-plan billing. Total setup is about $350–$450.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Fees below are approximate; confirm on the Texas Secretary of State and Comptroller sites before you file.

### Legal structure in Texas

**Default: single-member LLC (about $300 to file).** You'll be signing contracts and hosting other people's businesses; an LLC keeps a client dispute from reaching your personal savings, and it reads as more established on a proposal. If $300 isn't possible yet, start as a sole proprietor with an assumed name (DBA) certificate from the Dallas County Clerk, then convert to an LLC by client #2.

- [ ] Pick a name. Search it on Texas SOSDirect, the USPTO trademark search, and as a `.com`
- [ ] File the Certificate of Formation (Form 205) for an LLC online through SOSDirect (about $300)
- [ ] Get a free EIN from IRS.gov (10 minutes, use it instead of your SSN on forms)
- [ ] Open a free business checking account (local credit union, or an online bank such as Mercury or Novo)
- [ ] Calendar the annual Texas franchise tax Public Information Report, due May 15. Small LLCs owe no franchise tax below the revenue threshold, but the report is still required
- [ ] Ask the Texas Comptroller (or a CPA) whether you need a sales tax permit. Texas treats some data processing and hosting as taxable, so hosting in the care plan may be taxable even if design work is not. Settle this before billing your first care plan
- [ ] Set aside 25–30% of profit for federal taxes (self-employment tax is 15.3%; Texas has no state income tax). Pay quarterly estimates (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15) once you expect to owe $1,000+ for the year
- [ ] Required before the first Growth client (Hector, 2026-10-24): professional liability (E&O) insurance, often $30–$60/mo for a solo web designer
- [ ] One-time: pay a Texas small-business attorney (about $300–$600) to review your contract template. Cheaper than one bad dispute

### Contract essentials

One master contract plus a short project-specific scope page. Every contract covers:

1. **Scope:** package name, exact page list, features, number of revision rounds, what is *not* included
2. **Timeline:** turnaround in business days starting when content is complete; client content due date
3. **Payment:** 50/50 schedule for Launch and Growth; deposit refundable until the kickoff call (attorney to confirm); due dates, late fee (e.g. 1.5%/month after 15 days), work pauses on late payment
4. **Revisions and change orders:** what a round is; new requests quoted in writing first; hourly rate
5. **Client responsibilities:** content, accuracy of their claims and licenses, rights to photos and logos they send
6. **Ownership:** client owns the final site code, content and domain once the build fees are paid in full (unpaid care-plan fees stay owed but don't affect ownership); you keep the right to reuse your template and components, and to show the site in your portfolio
7. **Hosting and care plan:** what's included, monthly price, 30-day cancellation, what happens on cancellation (handoff)
8. **Abandonment:** if the client goes silent 30+ days, you may invoice the balance and close or launch the project
9. **Warranty:** 30 days of free bug fixes after launch (bugs, not new requests)
10. **Limitation of liability:** capped at fees paid; no guarantee of search rankings or revenue
11. **Termination:** either side with written notice; deposit fully refundable until the kickoff call, non-refundable after (attorney to confirm); work done is paid for at $75/hour (client owes the greater of the deposit or hours worked, never more than the project total)
12. **Governing law:** Texas, Dallas County

### Domain and hosting ownership policy

Put this in your contract and on your website's FAQ. Clients trust it, and it ends the most common web-designer horror story.

- **The client always owns their domain.** They register or keep it in *their own* registrar account (Cloudflare Registrar, Porkbun or Namecheap; avoid GoDaddy upsells). They add you as a delegate or share DNS access. You never register a client's domain in your name.
- **The client owns their Google Business Profile and Google Search Console.** You're added as a manager or user, never the only owner. Visitor stats come from Vercel Web Analytics on your Vercel team (section 5), so the client gets them from you on request, not as an account they own.
- **You host the site on your Vercel Pro team while they're on a care plan.** Hosting is included in the plan, not resold separately.
- **The code transfers on request** once the project is paid in full: you transfer the GitHub repo to their account and help them move hosting (one-time $150 handoff fee).
- **Logins live in a password manager** (Bitwarden free) using shared items, never in email or texts.
- **Before launch, the site stays on a preview link**, so final payment always comes before the domain points to it.

## 7. Client acquisition: first 3 clients in 30 days

Reach 120 hand-picked local businesses in 30 days at the section 2 prices, about 1–1.5 hours a day around class and work. Expect roughly 120 contacts → 15 replies → 6 discovery calls → 3 signed. Track every contact in one spreadsheet (`business/sales/prospect-tracker.csv`): business, niche, area, channel, date contacted, status, next follow-up, notes. Status is always one of: Contacted, Follow-up 1, Follow-up 2, Replied, Call booked, Proposal sent, Signed, Not interested, Opted out, No reply (closed).

**Default channel mix:** walk-ins first (highest reply rate for local owners), then email and DMs to the same list. Your warm network goes first of all: first clients often come from a referral like "my cousin's barber needs a site."

### Week 1 (days 1–7): get ready to sell

- [ ] Pick 2 niches (default: home trades + salons/barbers) and 3 areas you can reach easily (e.g. Oak Cliff, Garland, Richardson)
- [ ] Build the starter template (section 5)
- [ ] Build 2 demo sites, one per niche, for made-up businesses clearly labeled "Demo" (e.g. "Demo: North Texas HVAC"). They prove your work without inventing clients
- [ ] Put up your own 1-page site: packages, the 2 demos, PageSpeed scores, contact form
- [ ] Set up business email, a Google Business Profile for yourself, a Cal.com booking link, and a one-page PDF price sheet
- [ ] Text or message 20 people you know: "I'm starting a web design business for local businesses. Do you know an owner whose site needs help?"

### Week 2 (days 8–14): build the list and start outreach

- [ ] Build a list of 120 prospects from Google Maps: search "HVAC Garland," "barber Oak Cliff," etc. Keep those with 20+ reviews (they have money and customers) and no website, a Facebook-only page, or a site that scores under 50 on mobile PageSpeed
- [ ] For the top 20, record a 2-minute Loom on your phone: their site on mobile, the PageSpeed score, 3 specific fixes
- [ ] Walk in to 5 businesses per weekday (25 total); best times are mid-morning Tue–Thu, never during a rush
- [ ] Send 10 personalized emails or DMs per day (50 total)

### Week 3 (days 15–21): follow up and get calls

- [ ] Follow up everyone from week 2 once (scripts below); most replies come from the follow-up
- [ ] 25 more walk-ins and 25 more emails/DMs
- [ ] For every warm reply, build a free 1-page homepage preview using their real info on an unlisted, no-index link. Show it on the call; take it down if they pass
- [ ] Run discovery calls; send proposals within 24 hours

### Week 4 (days 22–30): close

- [ ] Follow up every proposal on day 2 and day 5
- [ ] Final follow-up to everyone who hasn't replied
- [ ] Sign contracts, collect deposits, send intake forms
- [ ] Write down what worked: which niche, which channel, which line got replies. Log it in the decisions table at the top

Best for salons, barbers, detailers and trainers who post often. Follow them and like a couple of real posts first.

### Rules for outreach

- Personalize every message with one real detail (a review, a post, their area). Never mass-blast.
- Two follow-ups maximum, then stop. Honor every "no" and opt-out immediately.
- Emails include your real name, business name, a mailing address (PO box or virtual mailbox) and a way to opt out.
- Never claim results you haven't produced ("I'll get you on page 1"). Say what you'll build and how you'll measure it.

### Private preview for a shop with no website

From the same design guide (section 5, design toolkit; decisions log, 2026-10-21). For a shop that clearly deserves a site, a private preview lets the owner see their own site before they hire you.

- **Pick:** 4.5 stars or more, 150+ Google reviews, no website (search the name to be sure), and one clear product or service.
- **Build it in an evening from the template:** their real name, hours and services from their Google profile, colors from their logo, and openly licensed or generic photos. No AI photos of their products, food or work, no reviews copied onto it, and no prices you haven't checked with them.
- **Keep it private:** a Vercel preview behind the login, noindex, never linked or posted anywhere, never on a public address.
- **Show it in person** on your phone (walk-in script above). Then quote at the section 2 prices, and build nothing more until they've signed.
- **If they say no,** delete the preview and its repo that week.

## 8. Pricing and income math

Redone 2026-10-24 for the section 2–3 prices. At 10 care-plan clients plus 1.5 new builds a month, you'd take home about $1,380 a month after costs and taxes, on roughly 12 hours a week: about $26 per hour. That's less than half of what the old prices paid per hour. The low build prices are most of the gap, so revisit them after the first 3 clients (below).

**Assumptions** (change them as real numbers come in):

- "Active clients" = clients paying the care plan. New builds are added on top at the pace shown.
- Average build = $800 (mostly Growth from $999, some Launch from $499). The real price is set in each proposal.
- Care plan $49/mo per client, plus about 30 minutes of billed edits per client per month at $75/hr ($37.50).
- A build takes about 28 hours including sales, calls and admin. Care takes about 1 hour per client per month (30 minutes of upkeep, 30 minutes of edits).
- Costs: Claude ($20, then $100 from 5 clients), Vercel Pro $20, Google Workspace $8, insurance $40 from 10 clients, plus about 3% card fees.
- Taxes: 25% of profit set aside for federal income and self-employment tax (no Texas state income tax).

**What the table says:**

- **Recurring revenue is your safety net.** If you stop selling for a semester (finals, internship), 10 care clients still bring in about $865 a month with their edits, for about 10 hours a month.
- **Edits are billed, not included.** Every edit is quoted at $75/hr in 30-minute blocks, so a client who needs lots of changes pays for that time instead of eating into a flat fee.

### When to raise prices

- After the first 3 clients: revisit Launch's portfolio-builder price (decisions log, 2026-10-23).

- When you're booked 4+ weeks out: raise prices 15% for new clients. A waitlist means you're underpriced.
