# Master service agreement

> **⚠ Template only, not legal advice.** Have a Texas small-business attorney review this once before you use it with any client (playbook §6, about $300–$600). Remove this box and every review note after review.
>
> **How it fits with the scope page:** each client signs this agreement once. Each project or care plan then gets its own project scope page (`contract-scope-template.md`), signed by both sides. This agreement holds the terms that are the same for every client. The scope page holds the details for one project: package, pages, prices, payment schedule, dates and care plan. Sections 1–12 here line up with sections 1–12 on the scope page, so each pair covers the same playbook §6 contract essential.

This master service agreement (the "Agreement") starts on ________ (the "Effective Date") and is between:

- **Designer:** Trinity Web Co. [LLC name if formed: ________], a ________ *(Texas limited liability company / sole proprietorship)*, with its address at ________ ("Designer"), and
- **Client:** ________ *(legal business name)*, a ________ *(type of entity)*, with its address at ________ ("Client").

## How this agreement works

- **Project scope pages.** Each project or care plan is described in a project scope page signed by both parties (a "Scope"). Each signed Scope becomes part of this Agreement.
- **If they conflict.** For one project's package, page list, features, prices, payment schedule, dates and care plan, the Scope controls. For everything else, this Agreement controls.
  > **⚖ Attorney review:** confirm this order of precedence. In particular, a Scope should not be able to weaken sections 9 (warranty), 10 (limitation of liability) or 12 (governing law) by accident.
- **Definitions.**
  - "Site" means the website Designer builds for Client under a Scope.
  - "Client Content" means anything Client provides: text, logos, photos, reviews, prices, licenses and other information.
  - "Designer Tools" means Designer's own starter templates, components, code libraries, build scripts and know-how, which Designer had before the project or uses across clients.
  - "Launch" means the day Client's domain first points to the Site.
  - "Business day" means Monday through Friday, excluding US federal holidays.
  - "In writing" includes email from the addresses listed on the Scope, unless this Agreement says a signature is needed.

## 1. Scope

1.1 Designer will build the Site described in the Scope: the package, the exact page list, the features, the number of revision rounds and any add-ons.

1.2 Anything not listed in the Scope is out of scope. This includes e-commerce, paid ads, logo design, ongoing content after Launch and third-party fees, unless the Scope or the care plan says otherwise.

1.3 Out-of-scope work only happens through a written change order (section 4).

## 2. Timeline

2.1 The build turnaround in the Scope is counted in business days. **It starts on the day Designer has received all Client Content**, not the day the Scope is signed.

2.2 Client will send all Client Content by the content due date in the Scope.

2.3 If Client is late with content, feedback, approvals or payments, the timeline moves back by at least the same number of days. Designer may also move the project behind work for other clients that is already scheduled.

2.4 Dates in the Scope are good-faith estimates. Designer will tell Client promptly if a date is going to slip.

## 3. Payment

3.1 **Fees and schedule.** Client pays the fees in the Scope on the schedule in the Scope. Under the playbook, the schedule is 50/50 for Starter and Growth, 40/30/30 for Premium, and 100% at signing for a founding Starter.

3.2 **Deposit.** Work starts only after the deposit has cleared. The deposit holds Client's place in Designer's schedule.
- If Client cancels **before the kickoff call**, Designer refunds the deposit in full.
- If Client cancels **after the kickoff call**, the deposit is not refundable.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** confirm that this refund rule is enforceable as written and appears the same way here, in section 11 and on the Scope.
>
> **✎ Hector to decide:** for a project paid 100% at signing (founding Starter), how much counts as the "deposit" if Client cancels after kickoff? Options: ☐ the full payment ☐ 50% of it, with the rest refunded minus completed work.

3.3 **Final payment before Launch.** The final payment is due before Designer points the domain at the Site. Until then, the Site stays on a preview link.

3.4 **How to pay.** Client pays by card or ACH bank transfer through Designer's payment processor (currently Stripe). Invoices are due on the dates in the Scope.

3.5 **Late payments.** A balance more than 15 days overdue accrues the late fee in the Scope. While any payment is overdue, Designer may pause all work, including care-plan work.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** the playbook's example late fee is 1.5% a month (18% a year). Confirm it fits Texas usury limits, and whether to call it a late fee or interest.

3.6 **Percentage add-ons.** Percentage add-ons (Spanish version, rush delivery) are calculated on the package price Client actually pays.

3.7 **Taxes.** Prices don't include sales tax. If Texas sales tax applies to any part of the work (for example, hosting in a care plan), Designer adds it to the invoice at the rate shown on the Scope.

> **⚖ Attorney review (or CPA):** confirm with the Texas Comptroller whether care-plan hosting is taxable, and how to show tax on the Scope (playbook §6).

## 4. Revisions and change orders

4.1 **Revision rounds.** The Scope sets how many revision rounds are included. One round is a single consolidated list of changes, sent by Client's approver within 5 business days of a review link. Designer turns around each round in about 2–3 business days.

4.2 **What isn't a revision.** New pages, new features or a new design direction are not revisions. They are change orders.

4.3 **Change orders.** Designer quotes every change order in writing. Work on it starts only after Client approves the quote in writing. An approved change order may change the price and the timeline.

4.4 **Rates.** An extra revision round is $150. Other extra work is billed at $75 an hour in 30-minute blocks, quoted first. These are the rates on the Scope, and the Scope controls if they differ.

4.5 **After the last round.** After the final included round, further changes are made under the care plan or billed at the hourly rate.

## 5. Client responsibilities

Client will:

5.1 Send all Client Content by the content due date, in the shared folder Designer provides.

5.2 Make sure every claim, price, license, certification, award and review on the Site is true, current and allowed by law. Designer publishes reviews and testimonials only as Client provides them, word for word, with the attribution Client gives.

5.3 Own, or have permission to use, every photo, logo, font and piece of text Client sends. Client is responsible if it doesn't.

5.4 Name one approver, who sends consolidated feedback and the written "approved for launch" before Launch.

5.5 Give Designer the access needed to do the work: domain DNS, Google Business Profile, booking tool and similar accounts. Client shares logins through a password manager, never by email or text.

5.6 Tell Designer what the Site's privacy policy needs to say, and confirm any disclaimers Client's industry requires. Designer provides a plain-language privacy policy page from what Client tells it. Designer is not a lawyer, and Client is responsible for whether that page is legally sufficient for Client's business.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** check that 5.6 fairly places responsibility for the privacy policy and industry disclaimers on Client.

## 6. Ownership

6.1 **What Client owns.** Once Client has paid every amount due under a Scope, Client owns the final Site built under it: its code, the content created for it, and the domain. Until then, Designer keeps all rights in the work, and Client may not use it outside the preview link.

6.2 **Designer Tools.** Designer keeps ownership of Designer Tools, even when they're part of the Site. Once Client has paid in full, Client gets a permanent, royalty-free, non-exclusive license to use, copy and change the Designer Tools inside the Site, including after a handoff. Client may not resell the Designer Tools on their own. Designer may reuse Designer Tools, and the general know-how from the project, for other clients.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** the playbook says the client "owns the final site code" and that Designer can reuse its template and components. Sections 6.1 and 6.2 try to do both. Confirm they don't conflict, and whether Texas law needs specific wording to assign copyright (6.1).

6.3 **Third-party materials.** Stock photos, fonts, plugins and embedded tools stay under their own licenses. Designer uses only materials licensed for commercial use, and tells Client about any license terms Client needs to follow.

6.4 **Client Content.** Client keeps ownership of Client Content. Client gives Designer permission to use it to build, host, maintain and show the Site.

6.5 **Portfolio.** Designer may show the finished Site, its URL and screenshots in Designer's portfolio and marketing. Designer will only publish a testimonial or case study that Client wrote or approved, attributed the way Client approves.

6.6 **Domain and accounts.** Client always owns the domain, registered in Client's own registrar account. Designer never registers a client domain in its own name. Client also owns its Google Business Profile, Google Search Console and analytics accounts. Designer is added as a manager, never as the only owner.

## 7. Hosting and care plan

7.1 **Hosting.** While the Site is on a care plan, Designer hosts it on Designer's hosting account (currently Vercel Pro). Hosting is included in the care plan, not sold separately. Every Site Designer hosts must be on a care plan.

7.2 **Included months.** Each build includes the first 3 months of Care Standard, founding prices included. The included months have no separate cash value and aren't refunded if Client cancels them early.

7.3 **Renewal.** After the included months, the care plan chosen on the Scope starts automatically at its monthly price, unless Client cancels in writing before it starts. Designer charges it by card or ACH on the same day each month.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** confirm that this automatic renewal is disclosed clearly enough for a business client in Texas. Ask whether the Scope should have a separate checkbox or initials next to the renewal terms.

7.4 **What's included.** Each plan includes what the Scope lists (from playbook §3), including the content-edit time and response time. Response times are in business days and refer to Designer's first reply and start of work, not finished work.
- Unused edit time doesn't roll over.
- Work beyond the plan is billed at $75 an hour in 30-minute blocks, quoted first.

7.5 **No uptime promise.** Designer monitors the Site and works to fix outages within the plan's response time. Designer doesn't guarantee uptime, and isn't responsible for outages caused by the hosting provider, the domain registrar, Client's form or booking tools, or other third parties, beyond working with them to fix the problem.

7.6 **Late care-plan payments.** If a care-plan payment fails and isn't fixed within 15 days, Designer may pause edits and other care-plan work. Designer will not take the Site offline for non-payment without at least ____ days' written notice.

> **✎ Hector to decide:** whether you'll ever take a site offline for non-payment, and after how many days' notice. Taking a client's site offline hurts their business and your reputation; most designers pause edits and send a final notice first.

7.7 **Cancellation.** Either party may cancel a care plan with 30 days' written notice.

7.8 **Handoff.** When a care plan ends, or Client declines one after the included months, Designer will:
- Transfer the Site's GitHub repository to Client's own account.
- Help move hosting to an account Client controls.
- Charge a one-time handoff fee of $150.

Designer does the handoff only once all amounts due are paid. After the handoff, Designer has no further duty to host, maintain or fix the Site.

7.9 **Price changes.** Designer gives at least 60 days' written notice before raising a care-plan price.

## 8. Abandonment

8.1 The project is "abandoned" if Client stops responding for 30 days or more, or is more than 30 days late with Client Content.

8.2 If the project is abandoned, Designer may invoice the remaining balance under the Scope and either close the project or launch the Site with the content on hand. Designer will send a written warning at least 7 days before doing either.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** check that invoicing the full remaining balance on abandonment is enforceable and not a penalty. The 7-day warning isn't in the playbook. It's here as a fairness step, so Hector should confirm it.

## 9. Warranty

9.1 **30-day bug fixes.** For 30 days after Launch, Designer fixes bugs at no charge. A bug is something that doesn't work the way it was built and approved. New requests, content changes, and problems caused by third-party services or by changes anyone other than Designer made are not bugs.

9.2 **No guaranteed results.** **Designer does not guarantee search rankings, traffic, calls, leads or revenue.**

9.3 **Accessibility.** Designer builds with common accessibility practices and checks the Site before Launch. Designer doesn't guarantee that the Site meets any accessibility law or standard, or that it will prevent an accessibility claim.

9.4 **No other warranties.** Except as stated in this section, Designer provides the Site and services "as is," without other warranties, express or implied.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** 9.3 and 9.4 need the right wording and formatting (for example, bold or all caps) to be enforceable in Texas. Also ask whether to include a Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act waiver; such waivers usually need the client to have its own lawyer, which most of these clients won't.

## 10. Limitation of liability

10.1 **Cap.** Designer's total liability for any claim under this Agreement is limited to the fees Client paid under the Scope the claim relates to. For a care plan, the limit is the care-plan fees Client paid in the 12 months before the claim.

10.2 **No indirect damages.** Neither party is liable for indirect, incidental or consequential damages, including lost profits, lost revenue or lost data.

10.3 **Client indemnity.** Client will defend and cover Designer against claims that arise from Client Content, or from Client's own products, services, claims or business practices shown on the Site.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** the playbook sets only "capped at fees paid." Confirm:
> - the 12-month care-plan window in 10.1
> - whether 10.2 should apply to both sides or only to Designer
> - whether the indemnity in 10.3 should be mutual
> - how all of this fits with professional liability (E&O) insurance, which §6 says is required before Premium clients

## 11. Termination

11.1 **Ending a project.** Either party may end a project with written notice.

11.2 **Deposit.** The deposit rule in section 3.2 applies: it's fully refundable before the kickoff call and not refundable after it.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** same wording as 3.2. Confirm both stay identical.

11.3 **Work done.** If a project ends after the kickoff call, Client pays for the work completed up to the termination date, at $75 an hour, minus payments already made. Client never pays more than the Scope total.

> **✎ Hector to decide:** the playbook says "work done is paid for" but doesn't say how to measure it. Hourly is the simplest to defend; the other option is the milestone payments already reached.

11.4 **Ownership on termination.** Client gets rights under section 6 only in work that has been paid for.

11.5 **Ending this Agreement.** Either party may end this Agreement with written notice once no Scope or care plan is active. Ending a care plan follows section 7.7.

11.6 **What survives.** Sections 3 (amounts already owed), 6, 7.8, 9.2–9.4, 10, 12 and 13 survive the end of this Agreement.

## 12. Governing law and disputes

12.1 This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Texas. Venue is Dallas County, Texas.

12.2 Before filing any claim, each party will try in good faith to settle the dispute by talking it through, for at least 15 days after written notice of the problem.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** confirm venue wording. Ask whether to add a clause for attorney's fees for the winning party, or mediation before court. Neither is in the playbook.

## 13. General terms

13.1 **Independent contractor.** Designer is an independent contractor, not Client's employee, partner or agent. Designer chooses how and when to do the work, within the Scope's dates.

13.2 **Confidentiality.** Each party keeps the other's non-public business information, logins and customer data private. Each uses it only for this Agreement, and shares it only with people or services that need it to do the work. This doesn't cover information that is public or that a party already had.

13.3 **Customer data.** Form submissions from the Site go to Client's inbox. Designer accesses Client's customer data only as needed to build, test and maintain the Site, and never sells it.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** check 13.2 and 13.3 against Texas data-privacy law for small businesses. Also check what Designer must do if a third-party service that handles form data has a breach.

13.4 **Third-party services.** The Site may rely on services such as the hosting provider, the domain registrar, and form, booking and analytics tools. These services have their own terms and fees, which Client pays unless the Scope or care plan says otherwise.

13.5 **Delays outside anyone's control.** Neither party is responsible for delays caused by events outside its reasonable control, such as outages at a third-party service, severe weather, illness or a natural disaster. Payment obligations still apply.

13.6 **Notices.** Notices go by email to the addresses on the most recent Scope. A party may update its address by notice.

13.7 **Whole agreement.** This Agreement and the signed Scopes are the whole agreement between the parties. They replace earlier proposals and conversations about the same work.

13.8 **Changes.** Changes to this Agreement need to be in writing and signed by both parties. Change orders follow section 4.

13.9 **Assignment.** Neither party may transfer this Agreement without the other's written consent. The one exception: Designer may transfer it to an LLC or other business entity Designer forms or owns.

13.10 **Severability.** If a court finds part of this Agreement unenforceable, the rest still applies, and the court may limit that part as needed rather than strike it.

13.11 **Signatures.** Electronic signatures and signed copies (PDF or e-sign tool) count as originals.

> **⚖ Attorney review:** check 13.9. If Hector signs before forming the LLC, confirm this lets him move existing clients to the LLC later.

## Attorney review list

2. **Deposit refund rule:** fully refundable before the kickoff call, non-refundable after (3.2 and 11.2, and on the Scope).
