# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

Trinity Web Co. as a product, for design work on the website (`site/`) and the Instagram kit (`business/marketing/instagram/`). `business/playbook.md` is the source of truth for how the business runs; when this file and the playbook disagree, the playbook wins. Confirmed with Hector on 2026-10-20.

## Platform

web

## Users

Owner-operated Dallas small businesses: home-service trades (HVAC, plumbing, electrical), barbers, salons and studios, and, on Instagram only, small restaurants. The owner decides alone, often has no website, a Facebook-only page or a slow old one, and sees posts and sites on a phone between jobs or customers.

## Product Purpose

Trinity Web Co. builds fast, custom-coded websites for these businesses, then keeps them running on a monthly care plan. The website and the Instagram account exist to earn a first conversation: a DM "CHECK" for a free site check, or a free 20-minute call. Success is Dallas owners who follow, then ask for that check.

## Positioning

A local developer you can text, not an agency. Starting prices posted openly, the exact price agreed in writing. The client owns their domain, Google Business Profile and accounts. Hand-built sites that load fast on a phone, looked after every month.

## Operating Context

Hector runs the business solo and part-time. Posts are built from the HyperFrames kit in `business/marketing/instagram/` from scripts in the "Instagram content plan" doc, in English first and then Spanish after Hector checks each script, three feed pieces a week on the "Instagram growth plan" schedule. Stickers (polls, questions, links) are added in the Instagram app when a story posts.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Prices, packages, care plans and add-ons come only from `business/playbook.md` §2–3 (in the kit, through `config.ts`).
- The website isn't live yet: the site and both demos are previews behind a Vercel login, so posts ask for a DM, never "link in bio", until launch.
- The demos' mobile PageSpeed results (2026-10-26, production): Purple Martin 100 with a 1.1 s load, Good Oak 100 with a 0.9 s load. They live in the kit's `config.ts`.
- The name stays Trinity Web Co.; the domain is trinitywebdallas.com (Hector, 2026-10-21), because trinitywebco.com belongs to someone else.
- A Spanish post is a translation, not an offer of a Spanish website (that's an add-on).
- Nobody appears on camera. Reel voiceovers are an ElevenLabs narrator who speaks for Trinity Web Co. and never names Hector; Instagram's AI info label goes on when they post.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Trinity Web Co.; Instagram @trinitywebco, in English and Spanish on one account.
- Voice: plain, friendly, specific, sentence case. Talk like you're at a shop owner's counter. No jargon, hype or fake urgency. Never promise rankings or results. Don't lead with AI.
- Assets: the Trinity River line drawing with Dallas neighborhoods, and the Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge logo with three cables.
- Visual specifics live in `site/src/styles/tokens.css` and the kit's `DESIGN.md`, not here.

## Evidence on Hand

- Two demo sites for made-up businesses, always labeled "Demo": Purple Martin Heating & Air (`clients/purple-martin-air-lake-highlands/site`) and Good Oak Barbershop (`clients/good-oak-barbers-oak-cliff/site`), with screenshots in each repo's `docs/screenshots/`.
- No clients, testimonials, reviews, case studies, follower counts or production speed scores exist yet. Never invent or imply any of them.

## Product Principles

1. Say the price. Every package and care plan price is public and fixed.
2. Teach before asking. Useful, specific advice comes first; the ask is one plain line at the end.
3. Show only what's real: labeled demos, real numbers, real clients once they exist.
4. The owner keeps control: their domain, their accounts, their decision.
5. Local and personal, not agency: Dallas places, plain words, a reply within 1 business day.
